# Manuscript integration for the calibrated full rerun

This is the replacement Methods and reporting plan for the **Computer Methods and Programs in Biomedicine** manuscript. Numerical Results and figures will be generated only from completed, checksum-verified outputs. The older 116-image development analysis remains historical evidence and must not be presented as the result of this rerun.

## Methods text

### Study datasets and eligibility

Public histopathology images and paired tissue annotations were obtained from BCSS (breast carcinoma), TIGER (breast carcinoma), IGNITE (non-small-cell lung carcinoma) and OCELOT (multiple organs). The acquisition manifests and source checksums were retained with the benchmark. Only calibrated images with a matched tissue-region annotation, at least 16 usable physical tile positions and at least two tissue groups were eligible for the controlled multiclass task. Nucleus-only annotations and fields without verified pixel spacing were not used as tissue-segmentation ground truth. Source labels were mapped to a frozen cross-dataset tissue vocabulary before scoring; unmapped and unannotated pixels were excluded from reference scores. OCELOT's cancer region was expert marked, whereas its broad non-cancer region was generated automatically. It was therefore evaluated as a separate tumor-positive control and was not pooled into multiclass intersection-over-union.

The registry contains 1,166 fields. The primary classifier training set contains 476 eligible fields from 172 distinct linked source IDs, and the primary evaluation set contains 60 fields from 60 known source IDs, 20 fields per dataset. Source IDs within BCSS and TIGER overlap and therefore their per-dataset counts must not be added to estimate distinct patients. Their split was linked across known duplicate patients and slides. No known source group was shared between the training and evaluation sets. Remaining cross-source linkage uncertainty was retained as a limitation; an ROI count was not represented as a patient count. Twelve prespecified IGNITE training fields with only one usable coarse group were excluded before fitting. No designated primary evaluation field failed the same preflight. The 60 evaluation fields had been inspected during method development; their results will be described as development evaluation rather than independent final testing.

### Physical preparation and tissue refinement

Images were resampled according to recorded pixel spacing for Virchow2 input at approximately 0.5 µm per pixel. Non-overlapping 224 × 224-pixel encoder tiles were used, with white padding at incomplete edges; no tile-image cache was retained after feature extraction. Full 16 × 16 × 1280 spatial tokens were saved with model revision, image checksum and per-case GPU cleanup receipts. Additional contexts at 1 and 2 µm per pixel were aligned by physical coordinates. The same frozen RGB9+19 evidence conversion and released Bora 0.2.1 Neighbor context classifier were used for every primary field. CellPhenotyper provides a user-facing route for calibrated Virchow2 features and coarse tile clusters; the controlled experiment instead used expert-derived coarse partitions, as described next.

For the controlled task, each expert tissue mask was reduced to a 90-pixel grid at approximately 0.5 µm per pixel. The majority annotated class in each grid tile was assigned an independently permuted anonymous group number. The expert identity key was withheld from the Bora predictor manifest and used only to make training targets and to score results. Bora refined all tissue supported by the H&E image, including tissue outside the annotated region. The released model assigned one tissue identity to each complete refined group. Final masks and GeoJSON polygons were exported at native image resolution. Polygon parts and enclosed holes below 20 native pixels were removed or filled in the final output, and the raster was regenerated from the accepted geometry.

### Outcomes and analysis

Pixel accuracy, classwise Dice and intersection-over-union, and equal-image mean macro intersection-over-union were measured only where expert tissue labels were available. Whole-group accuracy and class-balanced accuracy were assessed on groups with at least 50% annotated coverage and 80% target purity. Group accuracy was also stratified by the number of input tiles (1–4, 5–9, 10–19 and at least 20) to examine whether sparse groups provide weaker Virchow2 evidence. Direct tumor–stroma boundary F1 was measured within an 8-µm physical tolerance where an expert interface existed. Source-group bootstrap intervals retained all images from sampled known source groups. A reference-decoded coarse mask was scored as a privileged geometry diagnostic and was not treated as a deployable classifier. Virchow2 extraction and cold model loading were reported separately from Bora execution.

For OCELOT, expert tumor recall and the fraction of automatically identified broad non-cancer pixels called tumor were reported separately. The latter was not treated as a true expert false-positive rate. Multiclass macro intersection-over-union was not calculated for this source.

### Whole-slide and spatial-transcriptomics examples

Five calibrated 10x Genomics whole-slide images (colon cancer, pancreas, lymph node, tonsil and brain cancer) were encoded in bounded windows. The colon cancer slide has a supplied partial expert tissue GeoJSON that was withheld from inference and may be used within its verified annotated regions. The other four supplied GeoJSONs have heterogeneous or unresolved annotation provenance; they were retained for descriptive displays and will not be called expert ground truth or assigned accuracy scores without source verification. Three conventional Visium sections were analysed separately: breast cancer with Giotto, colorectal cancer with Seurat, and tonsil with SpatialExperiment. Gene-expression spots were clustered in their respective R workflows. A physically calibrated H&E tile centered on each spot was encoded with Virchow2, and the spot-derived groups were supplied as coarse labels to Bora. These sections do not have complete independent pixel-level expert tissue labels, so their refinements must be illustrated without assigning them a tissue IoU. The same spatial calibration rule was used for all H&E inputs; upsampling a coarser image was not interpreted as an increase in optical resolution.

A capped subset of 20 image–mask pairs was obtained from the BEETLE development release. Twenty fixed, nonoverlapping 3072 × 3072-pixel tissue fields were selected from 12 source slides and 12 patients; eight capped slides lacked an eligible field with two annotated tissue compartments. The slide TIFF resolutions corresponded to approximately 0.484–0.525 µm per pixel. The provider's slide inventory placed the selected patients at three clinical centers (six at SCH, four at NKI and two at RUMC); none of these selected slides came from BEETLE's TIGER or TCGA-BRCA subsets. Field selection used the expert masks, so this subset is a secondary development analysis rather than an independent test. BEETLE's invasive epithelium and necrosis labels have direct counterparts in the training vocabulary. Its broader non-invasive epithelium label does not have a verified exact counterpart and must be excluded from semantic accuracy; the provider's broad “other” label is likewise excluded. The public Grand Challenge evaluation annotations are sequestered and were not used. No BEETLE accuracy value is reported until encoding and scoring have completed.

## Results insertion gate

The primary Results text, Table 1 scores, group-size table, representative figure and all-case web gallery should be regenerated from `primary-summary.json`, all 60 per-case `score.json` files and their output checksums. The OCELOT control should be described only after its separate 512-field CPU assessment is complete. No numerical outcome should be inferred from a partial checkpoint or from the prior 116-image viewer.

## Required limitations

The controlled input was derived from expert tissue masks. Anonymous numbering concealed names but preserved the expert-informed spatial partition. The evaluated primary images were previously inspected; the new frozen split prevents known source-group leakage but is not an untouched prospective test. OCELOT's broad negative is not expert verified. BCSS and TIGER share known source material. Some provider source groups are not verified patient IDs. The single-slide and Visium examples evaluate applicability under different input types and cannot be pooled with the tissue-mask benchmark.
